# Style reference

Two running examples, and the distinction matters:

**A. A real record**, used so the patterns can be checked against a source that
actually exists: Herminia Ibarra, "Paving an alternative route: Gender
differences in managerial networks", *Social Psychology Quarterly*, 1997,
volume 60, issue 1, pages 91-102, DOI 10.2307/2787014.
**Single-authored.** Verified against Crossref on 2026-08-25.

**B. A synthetic two-author record**, used only where a rule depends on having
more than one author (the `&` versus `and` split, `et al.` thresholds):
`Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (2023). Title in sentence case. Journal Title,
45(2), 112-138.` **This is not a real paper.** Never copy it into a reference
list.

## APA 7 (psychology, education, communication, much of management)
```
Ibarra, H. (1997). Paving an alternative route: Gender differences in
    managerial networks. Social Psychology Quarterly, 60(1), 91-102.
    https://doi.org/10.2307/2787014
```
- Article title in **sentence case**; journal title in **title case** and italic
- Volume italic, issue in parentheses not italic, no space before it
- `&` before the final author in the list and inside parentheses; `and` in
  narrative text
- Up to 20 authors listed. At 21 or more: first 19, then `. . .`, then the last
- In-text: `(Ibarra, 1997)` · two authors, `(Author & Author, 2023)` · three or
  more authors use `et al.` from the **first** citation, `(Author et al., 2023)`
- Direct quote needs a page: `(Ibarra, 1997, p. 95)`
- DOI as `https://doi.org/...`, no `doi:` prefix, no period after
- Publisher location was **dropped** in APA 7. Books are `Publisher.` only
- Hanging indent 0.5 in, list titled **References**

## ASA 6th (sociology)
```
Ibarra, Herminia. 1997. "Paving an Alternative Route: Gender Differences in
    Managerial Networks." Social Psychology Quarterly 60(1):91-102.
    doi:10.2307/2787014.
```
- Year straight after the author, then the title in **quotation marks**, headline caps
- Journal italic, then `volume(issue):pages` with **no space** after the colon
- First author inverted, all subsequent authors in normal order
- **All** authors listed, however many
- In-text: `(Ibarra 1997)`, no comma before the year; page with a colon:
  `(Ibarra 1997:95)`. Two authors: `(Author and Author 2023)`
- Three or more authors: all names at first citation, `et al.` after
- List titled **References**

## APSA (political science)
Chicago author-date with minor deviations. Follow the APSA Style Manual;
when in doubt, Chicago author-date is the safe default.

## Chicago 17th/18th, author-date (history-adjacent social science, APSA base)
```
Ibarra, Herminia. 1997. "Paving an Alternative Route: Gender Differences in
    Managerial Networks." Social Psychology Quarterly 60 (1): 91-102.
    https://doi.org/10.2307/2787014.
```
- Note the **spaces**: `60 (1): 91-102`, unlike ASA
- In-text: `(Ibarra 1997, 95)` — a comma and a bare page number
- List titled **References**

## Chicago notes-bibliography (history, some qualitative work)
Footnote:
```
1. Herminia Ibarra, "Paving an Alternative Route: Gender Differences in
   Managerial Networks," Social Psychology Quarterly 60, no. 1 (1997): 95.
```
Bibliography entry inverts the first author and uses periods where the note
uses commas. Shortened note after the first: `Ibarra, "Paving," 95.`

## MLA 9 (humanities)
```
Ibarra, Herminia. "Paving an Alternative Route: Gender Differences in
    Managerial Networks." Social Psychology Quarterly, vol. 60, no. 1, 1997,
    pp. 91-102. JSTOR, https://doi.org/10.2307/2787014.
```
- Container logic: the journal is a container, the database is a second container
- In-text: `(Ibarra 95)` — author and page, **no year**
- Three or more authors: `Author, Alice A., et al.`
- List titled **Works Cited**

## Harvard
Not one style. Cite Them Right Harvard, and the house Harvard of dozens of
universities, differ in punctuation and in whether the year is bracketed. **Ask
which Harvard**, then get the institution's own guide. Do not assume.

## AMA 11 (medicine, public health)
```
1. Ibarra H. Paving an alternative route: gender differences in managerial
   networks. Soc Psychol Q. 1997;60(1):91-102. doi:10.2307/2787014
```
Numeric superscript in text. Journal abbreviated per NLM. Up to six authors,
then `et al`.

## IEEE
```
[1] H. Ibarra, "Paving an alternative route: Gender differences in managerial
    networks," Soc. Psychol. Q., vol. 60, no. 1, pp. 91-102, 1997.
```
Numeric in order of first citation, `[1]`.

## Conversion traps

| From → to | What breaks |
|---|---|
| Any numeric → any author-date | `[7] showed` has no name; sentences need rewriting, not just replacing |
| APA → ASA/Chicago/MLA | Sentence case must become headline case; proper nouns must survive |
| ASA → APA | `60(1):91-102` becomes `60(1), 91-102`; drop `doi:` for the https form |
| ASA/Chicago → APA | Author lists over 20 need the 19-ellipsis-last treatment |
| Anything → MLA | The year leaves the in-text citation entirely and a page number replaces it |
| APA → GB/T | Every entry needs a document-type marker added; there is no automatic source for it |
| Anything → Chicago notes | The in-text citations become footnotes, which changes the document, not just the strings |
