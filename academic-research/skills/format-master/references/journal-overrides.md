# Journal house overrides

**Always read the journal's current author guidelines.** The notes below are
common patterns, not a substitute for the source, and house rules change.

## The overrides that most often differ from the base style
- Abstract word limit, and whether it is structured
- Keywords: how many, and from a controlled vocabulary or free
- Whether the reference list caps at a number (some review journals cap at 60)
- Whether DOIs are required, forbidden, or optional
- Whether URLs are permitted for journal articles
- Footnotes versus endnotes, and whether substantive footnotes are allowed
- Tables and figures: at the end, or in place; separate files; caption position
- Anonymization for double-blind review: self-citations in the third person, or
  redacted to `[Author]` — and whether the reference entry is redacted too
- Data availability statement: required, and where it goes
- AI use disclosure: increasingly required, and the wording is often specified
- Ethics statement and IRB number placement
- Author contribution statement, usually CRediT taxonomy
- ORCID required for the corresponding author or for all authors

## By discipline, the usual base style
| Field | Base |
|---|---|
| Sociology | ASA |
| Political science | APSA (Chicago author-date) |
| Economics | Chicago author-date, journal-specific |
| Psychology | APA 7 |
| Education | APA 7 |
| Communication | APA 7, some Chicago |
| Management and organization studies | APA 7, with heavy house variation |
| Anthropology | Chicago, or AAA style |
| Public health | AMA or APA |
| Chinese journals | GB/T 7714-2015, 顺序编码制 unless stated |

## Anonymizing for double-blind review
1. Remove the title page and any acknowledgements.
2. Strip document metadata — Word keeps the author name in file properties, and
   this is the most common way anonymity is broken.
3. Self-citations: `Author (2021) argued...` is the usual instruction, but some
   journals want normal third-person citation with the entry left in the list.
   Follow the journal; over-anonymizing makes the paper unreadable.
4. Remove identifying details from the data description only if the journal asks
   — usually the institution can stay if it is the study site.
5. Check figures, appendices and supplementary files for names and file paths.
