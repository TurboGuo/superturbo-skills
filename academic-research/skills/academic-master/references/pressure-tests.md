# Pressure tests for an idea

Run every one. For each, the idea either survives with a reason, needs a design
change, or dies. Say which — a test you run and do not resolve is theatre.

## 1. The rival explanation
Name the single most plausible alternative cause of the outcome. If the study as
designed cannot separate the two, that is a design defect, not a limitation
paragraph.

## 2. Reverse causality
Could Y cause X? In social science it usually could. What in the design rules it
out: temporal order, an instrument, a shock, a panel, a qualitative sequence?

## 3. Selection
Who is in the sample and how did they get there? Does the selection mechanism
correlate with both X and Y? Selection on the dependent variable is still the
most common fatal flaw in case-based work.

## 4. Measurement
Is the construct the thing being measured, or a proxy the field has agreed to
stop arguing about? Name the gap between construct and indicator out loud.

## 5. Definitional truth
Would the hypothesis be true by definition if both constructs were measured with
the same instrument? "Engaged employees report higher engagement" is a
tautology wearing a lab coat.

## 6. Scope
What is the population the claim covers? A finding on 400 undergraduates at one
university is a finding about 400 undergraduates at one university until an
argument is made otherwise.

## 7. Already done
Has this been done? Search before asserting novelty. If close work exists, the
contribution moves to "what it gets wrong" or "where it does not travel", and
that is a stronger paper anyway.

## 8. So what
If the result came out exactly as predicted, what changes? If the honest answer
is "nothing", the design is fine and the question is not.

## 9. The null
If the result is null, is the paper publishable and interesting? If not, the
study is a bet on one outcome and the design should be reconsidered.

## 10. The reviewer with a grudge
Who in this field would want this paper rejected, and what would they say? Write
their sentence. Then answer it.
