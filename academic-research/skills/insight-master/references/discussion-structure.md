# Discussion structures by paper type

## Empirical quantitative (the default, 1,500-2,500 words)
1. Finding restated, one paragraph, no method recap
2. Interpretation against specific prior work — confirms X, complicates Y,
   contradicts Z, each with the paper named
3. Mechanism: why this happened, with the evidence or the argument, labelled
4. Boundary conditions: where it should and should not hold
5. Theoretical contribution
6. Practical implications, licensed by the design
7. Limitations, hardest first
8. Future research, derived from 7
9. Conclusion, 3-5 sentences, no new material

## Empirical qualitative
1. The core interpretation, stated as the reader's takeaway
2. Each theme developed with its strongest evidence, including the negative case
3. The theoretical model or process the themes constitute — often a figure, see
   theory-graph-master's `codetree` and `framework` modes
4. Dialogue with the literature: what this account adds that existing accounts
   cannot explain
5. Transferability: to which settings, and on what grounds
6. Reflexivity: how the researcher's position shaped the account
7. Implications
8. Limitations and future research

## Mixed methods
Integrate rather than report in sequence. The discussion is where the two
strands meet:
1. The joint finding, stated once
2. Where the strands converged, and what that supports
3. **Where they diverged, and what the divergence means** — this is the highest
   value paragraph in a mixed methods paper and it is usually missing
4. Meta-inferences
5. Contribution, implications, limitations

## Systematic review or meta-analysis
1. Summary of evidence, with the pooled estimate and its interval
2. Heterogeneity: what explains it, from the moderator analysis
3. Quality of the evidence base, and what that does to confidence
4. Publication bias assessment and its effect on the conclusion
5. What the field should now do differently, in research and in practice
6. Limitations of the review process itself, not only of the included studies

## Theoretical or conceptual paper
1. The framework restated
2. Propositions, each with its warrant
3. What the framework explains that existing frameworks cannot
4. What it predicts that is testable, and how
5. Boundary conditions
6. Research agenda, specific enough to be actionable

## Conclusion section, all types
Three to five sentences. The finding, the contribution, the one thing the reader
should take away. **No new citations, no new results, no new caveats.** If a
caveat belongs anywhere, it belongs in limitations.
