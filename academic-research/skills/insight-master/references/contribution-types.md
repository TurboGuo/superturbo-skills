# Contribution types

## Whetten's (1989) four questions, and which one you are answering
- **What** — which constructs belong in the explanation. A contribution here
  adds or removes a construct, and must justify comprehensiveness and parsimony.
- **How** — how the constructs relate. Direction, sign, form, sequence.
- **Why** — the underlying dynamic that justifies the how. **This is where most
  real theoretical contributions live**, and it is the question most papers skip.
- **Who, where, when** — the boundary conditions. Contributions here set the
  limits of an existing theory and are systematically undervalued by authors and
  valued by reviewers.

A paper that only answers "what" and "how" is a finding. Adding "why" makes it
theory. Adding "when" makes it a boundary contribution, which is often the most
defensible thing an early-career paper can claim.

## The practical typology

**New relationship.** A and B are connected and nobody looked. Strong only if
the reason nobody looked is interesting; otherwise it reads as arbitrary.

**New mechanism.** The established relationship runs through M, not through the
mechanism the field assumed. Requires evidence about M, not just a mediation
coefficient from cross-sectional data.

**New boundary condition.** The relationship reverses, disappears or amplifies
under C. Powerful because it reconciles conflicting findings and because it
tells other researchers where to stop generalizing.

**New construct.** The phenomenon needs a name. High risk: most new constructs
are old constructs renamed, and reviewers will check. Requires a definition,
a distinction from the nearest three existing constructs, and evidence that the
distinction matters empirically.

**Reconciliation.** Two literatures contradict each other and both are right
under conditions the field had not specified. Among the most satisfying
contributions available, and it usually requires reading two literatures that
do not cite each other.

**Refutation or failed replication.** A well-established finding does not hold.
Increasingly publishable, and requires more power and more care than the
original, not less.

**Methodological.** The field has been measuring or estimating it wrong.
Requires showing that the correction changes a substantive conclusion, not only
that the old method was imperfect.

**Context extension.** The relationship works differently in an under-studied
setting. **Weak on its own.** "We tested it in China" is a replication with a
new sample unless there is a theoretical reason the setting should change the
relationship — an institutional difference, a cultural mechanism, a different
opportunity structure. Name the reason and the contribution becomes a boundary
condition, which is a real contribution.

## Testing the claim before a reviewer does
- Can you name the specific paper or assumption your finding revises? If it is
  "the literature", the contribution is too diffuse.
- Would someone in the field disagree with your contribution sentence? If nobody
  would, it is not a contribution, it is a summary.
- Does the contribution survive if the effect size is half what you found?
- Is the contribution in the abstract, in a sentence, without the word
  "implications"?
